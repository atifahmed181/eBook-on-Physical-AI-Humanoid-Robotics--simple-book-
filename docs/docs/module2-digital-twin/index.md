# Module 2: The Digital Twin (Gazebo & Unity)

## Overview

In this module, we'll explore the concept of digital twins in robotics, focusing on physics simulation and environment building using Gazebo and Unity. A digital twin is a virtual replica of a physical robot that allows us to test and validate our algorithms in a safe, controlled environment before deploying them on real hardware.

Digital twins have become essential in robotics development, particularly for humanoid robots, which are expensive and potentially dangerous to test extensively in the physical world. In this module, we'll cover two leading simulation platforms: Gazebo for physics-based simulation and Unity for high-fidelity rendering and human-robot interaction.

### What You'll Learn

In this module, you'll gain hands-on experience with:
- Building and configuring physics simulations in Gazebo
- Simulating realistic environments with gravity, collisions, and physics
- Creating high-fidelity visual environments in Unity
- Simulating various robotic sensors (LiDAR, Depth Cameras, IMUs)
- Bridging between simulation platforms and real hardware

### Why Digital Twins?

Digital twins enable:
- Safe testing of complex algorithms without risk to hardware or humans
- Rapid prototyping and iteration of robot behaviors
- Synthetic data generation for AI training
- Development of control algorithms before hardware availability
- Stress testing of robot systems under various conditions

### Prerequisites

Before diving into this module, you should have:
- Basic understanding of robot kinematics and dynamics
- Familiarity with URDF from Module 1
- Basic knowledge of 3D modeling concepts
